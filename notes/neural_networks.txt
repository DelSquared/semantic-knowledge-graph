Neural Networks

Neural networks are computing systems inspired by biological neural networks that constitute animal brains. These systems learn to perform tasks by considering examples, generally without being programmed with task-specific rules.

A neural network consists of layers of interconnected nodes (neurons) that process information. Each connection has a weight that adjusts as the network learns. The learning process typically involves forward propagation of input data through the network, calculation of error at the output, and backpropagation of the error to adjust weights.

Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that uses neural networks with many layers (deep networks). Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are particularly effective for image recognition and computer vision tasks. Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and transformers are used for sequential data such as text and time series.

Neural networks have revolutionized many fields including computer vision, natural language processing, speech recognition, robotics, and autonomous systems. Large language models (LLMs) based on transformer architectures have demonstrated impressive capabilities in text generation and understanding.
